# 2. Observable BSD Overlap and Ownership Paradoxes

The BSD projection is intentionally not normalized.

Several Field expressions appear in multiple BSDs.

Examples:

- `Data Science`
  - BSD-COMP-AI
  - BSD-SWEDEV-INFOINSIG
  - BSD-BIZINNO-SERV
  - BSD-BIOHEAL-MEDTECH

- `Computer Science`
  - BSD-COMP-AI
  - BSD-SWEDEV-INFOINSIG
  - BSD-ROBOTICS-MECENG

- `Artificial Intelligence`
  - BSD-COMP-AI
  - BSD-ROBOTICS-MECENG

- `Computer Engineering`
  - BSD-COMP-AI
  - BSD-ROBOTICS-MECENG

These overlaps are not treated as modeling errors.

They expose the difference between stakeholder-oriented business
boundaries and academic disciplinary identity.

# 3. Academic-Based Field Set

The following Fields survived academic normalization in this Dry Run.

The set is deliberately one-dimensional.

No parent-child relationship, hierarchy, nesting, or disciplinary
topology is asserted at this stage.

## Computer Science

Canonical Identity:
`Computer Science`

Semantic Boundary:
A computing discipline centered on computational foundations,
algorithms, programming, abstraction, software, information
management, systems, and related computational methods.

Normalization:
The identity is independently recognized by computing curriculum
frameworks and dedicated accreditation criteria.

Key Provenance:
- ACM / IEEE-CS / AAAI — Computer Science Curricula 2023
- ABET / CSAB — Computer Science program criteria

---

## Software Engineering

Canonical Identity:
`Software Engineering`

Semantic Boundary:
An engineering-oriented computing discipline concerned with
systematic specification, design, construction, verification,
validation, evolution, and professional development of complex
software systems.

Normalization:
Expressions such as software development are not automatically
treated as equivalent Fields. The canonical identity is normalized
to Software Engineering because disciplinary and accreditation
frameworks establish an independent engineering-oriented knowledge
boundary.

Key Provenance:
- ACM / IEEE-CS — Computing Curricula
- ABET / CSAB / IEEE — Software Engineering program criteria

---

## Information Systems

Canonical Identity:
`Information Systems`

Semantic Boundary:
A computing discipline concerned with the development, delivery,
use, and management of information systems within organizational
and other domains of activity.

Normalization:
Information Systems remains distinct from Information Technology
because its academic and accreditation identity emphasizes
information systems operating within organizational environments.

---

## Information Technology

Canonical Identity:
`Information Technology`

Semantic Boundary:
A computing discipline concerned with selecting, developing,
applying, integrating, administering, and supporting computing
technologies and infrastructure for user and organizational needs.

Normalization:
The Field is retained independently from Computer Science and
Information Systems because established computing frameworks
recognize a distinct applied systems and infrastructure identity.

---

## Data Science

Canonical Identity:
`Data Science`

Semantic Boundary:
An interdisciplinary computing and quantitative Field concerned
with acquiring, managing, analyzing, modeling, and interpreting
data to derive knowledge.

Normalization:
Its repeated appearance across multiple BSDs is preserved in the
BSD projection but collapsed into one academic identity here.

This is a direct example of:

BSD multiplicity != Academic identity multiplicity

---

## Cybersecurity

Canonical Identity:
`Cybersecurity`

Semantic Boundary:
A computing Field concerned with protecting data, software,
components, connections, systems, people, organizations, and
societal infrastructure against risks and threats.

Normalization:
Dedicated curricular and accreditation structures support a stable
identity beyond cybersecurity merely being a technique used by
other computing Fields.

---

## Computer Engineering

Canonical Identity:
`Computer Engineering`

Semantic Boundary:
An engineering and computing Field concerned with processor-based
systems integrating hardware, software, communications, and related
computational components.

Normalization:
Its appearance in both Computing/AI and Robotics/Mechanical BSDs
does not create multiple academic identities.

## Environmental Science

Canonical Identity:
`Environmental Science`

Semantic Boundary:
An interdisciplinary natural and applied science Field integrating
physical, chemical, biological, earth, quantitative, and
human-impact knowledge for understanding environmental systems.

Normalization:
The BSD expression "Environmental Justice and Climate Science" is
not copied directly into the academic Field set.

Environmental Science has stronger formal disciplinary grounding
in the current evidence set.

Normalization:

R&D classifications recognize Environmental Engineering within

# 4. Unresolved Academic Expressions

The following BSD-observed expressions are deliberately NOT resolved
into final independent academic Field identities in this generation:

- Artificial Intelligence
- Robotics
- Climate Science
- Environmental Justice
- Medical Engineering
- Medical Biotechnology
- Economics and Business

This does not mean that these expressions are invalid disciplines.

It means that this generation does not yet have sufficient reason
to assert that each should occupy an independent node in the
one-dimensional normalized Field set rather than representing:

- a subfield,
- a knowledge area,
- a cross-disciplinary area,
- an alternative canonical naming,
- a specialization,
- or a future topology node.

Their relationships must be examined by a later academic-topology
generation process.

# 5. Dry-Run Interpretation

The generation exposes two different semantic spaces.

BSD-Based Space:

Stakeholder Boundary
→ Observation
→ Overlap
→ Duplication
→ Ownership Ambiguity
→ Classification Paradox

Academic-Based Space:

Evidence
→ Identity Resolution
→ Semantic Normalization
→ Provenance
→ Deduplication
→ One-Dimensional Field Set

The transformation therefore does not attempt to make the BSD
taxonomy academically clean.

Instead, it preserves the BSD taxonomy as an observable business
projection and derives a separate academically defensible Field
representation.

The one-dimensional Academic Field Set is intentionally not the
final curriculum ontology.

It is a normalized input boundary for a subsequent academic
topology reconstruction process.
